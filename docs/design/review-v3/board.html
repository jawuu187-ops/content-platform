The work,
not the frame.

OnlyFans is the benchmark, not a reference. People come to a creator subscription product to see the person and buy access in as few steps as possible. v3 had the right identity and the wrong composition: typography led, photography decorated, and the price sat one screen away from the decision. This revision keeps the brand and rebuilds the layout around the work.

What that means in practice: the photograph is the largest object on the screen, covers bleed to the card edge, the subscription price lives inside the button that sells it, tiers are comparable cards instead of a hairline list, and the browsing ground is a warm near-white so media separates from chrome without a rule around every row.

Composition, measured

Discover: photography is 40% of the first screenful, 80% including the second row. v3 was 20%.

Creator: cover plus a 6-cell grid; the membership strip is a preview, the comparison lives one tap away.

Price adjacency: “Join Studio · $9.00 / month” is one object on 02, 03, 04 and 08. Two taps from a profile to a closed checkout.

Unchanged: eclipse mark, VESPER wordmark, oxblood as the only action colour, Bodoni for names and decision prices, Plex Mono for money, bone chrome.

Stand-in photography

Four generated stand-ins used to test composition: a set needs more than one face to be believable. Licensed photography is a separate decision and has not been bought.

02Recomposition · what changed screen by screensame identity, product structure

The previous round was measured, and the measurement said the product had drifted into editorial: a date line opened Discover, seven of eighteen screens were hairline lists, one screen in eighteen used a real avatar, and price appeared inside a call to action three times. Those are the things a subscriber notices first, so they were rebuilt before anything else was touched.

Surface triad. Bone #F4EFE7 is now chrome, lists and billing. Browsing sits on a warmer near-white #FBF8F3. Cards are #FFFDFA at a 14 px radius with a 10% border, so media separates without a rule around every row. Inside a card the photograph runs edge to edge; a 3 px overlay carries the counter, the price or the lock.

Nothing was added to the product to make this work. Every composition below uses a route the backend already serves: profiles, tiers, entitlements, PPV unlocks, posts, delivery, ledger.

Wordmark and mark
Vesper

The mark is an eclipse: two circles, oxblood and paper. It survives at 16 px, prints in one ink, and never needs a gradient. The wordmark is set in the didone at 0.16 em tracking — the only place type is tracked that wide.

Vesper: the evening star. Adult without being explicit, and it reads as a place rather than a feature.

Four destinations: Home, Library, Create (studio, hidden for non-creators), You. Pushed screens keep one back path; unlocking and confirmation are sheets. Money is never more than one step from the thing it buys.

Before implementation

  1. Does this now read as a creator subscription product rather than a publication?
  2. Is the photograph large enough, and is the typing around it quiet enough?
  3. Is the price on the action the right amount of selling, or still too much?
  4. Does Studio leading with posting match how a creator actually works?
  5. What still feels like a design object rather than an app a subscriber uses?
